The Spark at 5,364 Meters

Why an overland teenage trek turned into a mission to make Thai mountaineering history.

The Overland Decision

Instead of booking a short flight straight into Lukla Airport, I committed to the true overland route—an intense, grueling 15+ hour off-road jeep drive from Kathmandu to Surke, followed by hiking up into Lukla on foot. Carrying my own full pack, I navigated the entire Khumbu Valley round-trip without a guide or porter, filming every honest moment for my YouTube channel, @noproblemrr.

"When I reached Everest Base Camp, I met a group of international climbers. Seeing a 16-year-old standing there alone, unguided, having trekked all the way up from Surke, they stopped me, handed me $200 from their own pockets, and said:
'Take this. Keep training, and become the youngest climber from Thailand to summit Mount Everest.'"

That moment transformed a solo endurance test into an unwavering mission. Those climbers didn’t just hand me $200—they gave me the conviction that with discipline, technical climbing mastery, and dedicated sponsors, a Thai teenager can stand on the top of the world.

🎯 Why Island Peak (April 2027)?

Reaching Everest isn't about rushing—it is about earned competence. Island Peak (Imja Tse, 6,189m) requires glacier travel, fixed-rope ascents, crampon footwork, and crevasse crossings. It is the crucial next technical step.

🧗 Why Ama Dablam (April 2028)?

Known as the "Matterhorn of the Himalayas," Ama Dablam (6,812m) is one of the most demanding technical rock, snow, and ice peaks on the planet. Mastering it proves readiness for Everest’s death zone.
