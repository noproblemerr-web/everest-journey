The Spark at 5,364 Meters

Why a solo teenage trek turned into a mission to make Thai mountaineering history.

How It Started

I set out on the Everest Base Camp trek independently—carrying my own pack, navigating the Khumbu Valley alone without a guide or porter, and documenting every raw, challenging moment for my YouTube channel, @noproblemrr.

"When I reached Everest Base Camp, I met a group of international climbers. Seeing a 16-year-old standing there alone and unguided, they stopped me, handed me $200 from their own pockets, and said:
'Take this. Keep training, and become the youngest climber from Thailand to summit Mount Everest.'"

That moment transformed a personal adventure into a lifetime calling. Those climbers didn’t just give me $200—they gave me the conviction that with discipline, relentless preparation, and the right partners, a Thai teenager can stand on the highest point on Earth.

🎯 Why Island Peak (April 2027)?

Reaching Everest isn't about rushing—it is about earned competence. Island Peak (Imja Tse, 6,189m) requires glacier travel, fixed-rope ascents, crampon footwork, and crevasse crossings. It is the crucial next technical step.

🧗 Why Ama Dablam (April 2028)?

Known as the "Matterhorn of the Himalayas," Ama Dablam (6,812m) is one of the most demanding technical rock, snow, and ice peaks on the planet. Mastering it proves readiness for Everest’s death zone.
